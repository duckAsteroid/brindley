# Why "Brindley"?

James Brindley (1716–1772) engineered Manchester's Bridgewater Canal — the start of Britain's
canal network. Three things are recorded about how he worked, and each one is in the tool.

## He designed fully before anyone dug

> "he worked out complex engineering problems in his head, sometimes retiring to bed for days
> while he mentally solved particularly difficult challenges"

An initiative isn't ready until its open questions are settled. The
[design-review prompt](/guide/workflow#_1-design-it) is the retiring-to-bed part — with your agent
for company.

## He went and looked first

> "He surveyed routes by eye and instinct rather than with sophisticated instruments, often lying
> down on hilltops to judge the lie of the land"

His notebook records setting out "for Chester and Shropshire survey or a raconitering" — his
spelling of a reconnoitre. That's a [spike](/guide/spikes): build just enough to measure the
ground before committing to the route. (`type: raconiter` works too.)

## He didn't write it down

> "his stubborn refusal to delegate or properly record his methods created problems for those who
> worked with him"

This is the habit the tool fixes. The design lives in the repo — every decision, every question
answered, every alternative ruled out — where the people and agents who come after can follow it.
